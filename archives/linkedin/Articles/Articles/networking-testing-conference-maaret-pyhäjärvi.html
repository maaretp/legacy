A Networking Testing Conference

Created on 2018-01-14 20:39

Published on 2018-01-14 20:58

I know testing conferences, at least some of them. I can safely say I've been to a few. The end of year reflection showed I've personally delivered 352 sessions as a speaker. I've been predominantly international for the last three years. Seeing enough made me start one of my own: European Testing Conference. In 2018 February 19-20th we are in Amsterdam and you're welcome to join.

As a socially challenged extrovert, I started speaking at conferences to overcome my dilemma of connecting. I love learning with people, through deep and meaningful discussions. But opening up a conversation gets blocked with an irrational fear: what if the other does not want to talk with me? What if I start talking about a topic they don't care for? So I wait for others to start, and then join in fully.

There's been awesome conferences that have "no one is alone" rule that actually feels it is working (TestBashes are wonderful). There's conferences that maximize the hallway discussions, making the whole conference one big open space, where you don't know what the sessions will be about but you can always guide that by proposing your own sessions.

We wanted a mix. A conference with a stream of steady talks from both seasoned and new (mentored) speakers to give us wide view into what testing looks like from both tester and programmer point of views. A conference with whole-conference sessions that would be enabling networking and learning from everyone.

The networking sessions are framed in three formats:

  1. Speed Meet is about meeting 10 people in 60 minutes. It takes place early on in the conference, making sure you have friendly faces you know to join when the first lunch time hits you. In order to even out the discussion, we facilitate the session with a mind map rule. You have a mind map of you and your discussion partner gets to choose what in your mind map is something they want to discuss on.
  2. Lean Coffee is about talking in small groups. Regardless of its name, it has little to do with coffee and a lot to do with making a backlog of topics, and discussing them in timeboxes until the group vote moves us on to the next topic. The way we've organized this session is that every table is facilitated by a speaker. So look around for your favorite speaker, and join that table.
  3. Open Space is about making everyone a speaker. While we think we did a good job in hand-picking the best of talks, we just couldn't include everything. In open space, everyone gets to propose sessions and have discussions on either things they want more info on that the conference inspired, or anything we missed. Our speakers often use this opportunity to try out topics they are prepping for conferences, and content is generally great.

So if your goal is to learn testing, as a tester or a programmer, you should join us in Amsterdam February 19-20th. Amsterdam Arena is a great location, and we look forward to learning with you.
