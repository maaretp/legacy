# Maaret Pyhäjärvi on LinkedIn: Themes

A thematic classification of the LinkedIn post history, covering May 2010 to
October 2026, from two sources in `archives/linkedin/`:

- the personal data export
  (`Complete_LinkedInDataExport_08-30-2026.zip/Shares_91689.csv`), and
- posts pulled from the Member Changelog API
  (`Shares_changelog_20260921-20261005.csv`).

## Scope

- 2,307 shares in total: 2,278 from the export, dated **2010-05-17 to
  2026-08-28**, and 29 from the changelog, dated **2026-09-21 to 2026-10-05**.
- **Posts from 2026-08-29 to 2026-09-20 are missing.** The changelog did not
  reach back that far and no newer export has been taken.
- 2,162 carry post text; the other 145 are re-shares or link shares with no
  commentary (most of them from 2022–2023).
- This digest classifies the 2,162 posts with text: about 1.45 million
  characters, median post length 587 characters, longest 3,508.
- Percentages are the share of posts touching a theme, from bilingual
  (English and Finnish) keyword matching, so they are approximate. Posts
  average three themes each, and about 6 percent matched none.
- The qualitative notes and quotes come from reading a sample of roughly 250
  posts spread across all years up to August 2026, plus all 29 posts from the
  changelog, not from reading every post.
- Comments, reactions, and reposts are not included; those are in the separate
  API snapshot, not in `Shares.csv`.

## Four eras

The archive is not one voice held steady for sixteen years. It changes shape
four times.

| Era | Posts | What LinkedIn is used for |
| --- | ----- | ------------------------- |
| 2010–2011 | 18 | Tweet-length one-liners on testing, cross-posted |
| 2012–2016 | 271 | Group discussion posts with a title line: event announcements, enrolment links, and questions to Agile Finland and Finnish testing communities. Half in Finnish |
| 2017–2019 | 80 | Sparse, short, English: conference promotion and links to articles |
| 2020–2026 | 1,793 | The feed as a thinking-out-loud space: reflective posts that grow longer over the years (median 392 characters in 2020, 699 in 2026), with Finnish returning from 2022 |

Volume jumps in 2020 (147 posts, up from 25) and peaks in 2024 (411). The
busiest months are May 2024 (65 posts) and August 2024 (60).

## Core themes

### 1. Testing craft and quality — about 64 percent

The constant. The very first posts in 2010 are already the argument of the next
sixteen years in miniature:

- "If one get paid more for 'planning' testing = documenting than testing,
  exploratory testing may have difficulties." (2010)
- "V-model confusion: system test without an end-to-end system even though one
  is available." (2010)

Recurring positions:

- **Testing is not one thing.** "Testing != testing. Your testing is not my
  testing. They are all slices"; "Testing is not the intent. Intent is more
  fine-grained."
- **Developers test; testers are not their assistants.** "Today I am a testing
  radical. What if we paid less for the developers who can't test their own
  thing?" Testers would have time for the fine-grained work "if they didn't end
  up as developer's personal assistants". In 2025: organisations that let go of
  testers "don't let go testing"; it is "for *testing to remain* but be
  differently distributed".
- **Impatience with word wars.** "Checking is testing. It's not all there is to
  testing but it is testing."
- **Named concepts introduced along the way**: soak testing ("three categories
  of soak testing: none, days, weeks"), errors of omission, the buyer's
  classification of contemporary exploratory testing / "manual automated
  testing" / manual exploratory testing, and the two challenges of testing
  ("not allocating space for it" and "results due to skills in the space
  allocated").
- **The one kind disliked**: "I don't like testing we do for quality systems
  compliance. The kind that starts with requirement and ends with a linked test
  case as proof."
- **Autumn 2026, sharper still**: "Just stop with the test cases. No, I don't
  want them generated, nor do I want to read them." *Manual test automation* as
  a pet peeve: automation stays manual when humans stick "to roles of trigger,
  operator and parser". And in Finnish: testing is too important to be left
  only to testers, and too important to be left without testers.
- **Bug stories from daily life**, such as the food-delivery app that delivered
  a second meal for free, reported twice.

### 2. Exploratory testing — about 15 percent

Peaks in 2020–2021 (25 percent) around the *Exploratory Testing Foundations*
course and the *Contemporary Exploratory Testing* label (80 posts from 2021 on).

- **Agency and intent** are the two words used to define it: "it's all about
  agency - who makes choices of how we test"; charters are "the tool that helps
  us with intent"; "You don't need to choose right, because you get to choose
  again."
- **The head, not the hands.** "You can't see what my head does by watching
  where my hands go." "Dull work is like a shower, giving just enough
  inspiration through my hands to get my brain to do a second level of
  thinking." Five years later, after half an hour of watching automation run
  and finding what it did not cover: "Tuijottaminen ei ole tylsää kun oikeita
  asioita tapahtuu pään sisällä" (staring is not boring when real things happen
  inside your head).
- **Automation is part of it, not its opposite.** "There's a lot of insights
  from exploratory testing captured on those programmatic tests, and that is
  #ContemporaryExploratoryTesting" — next to release numbers: lead time from
  last commit 7 hours down to 19 minutes, 275 integrated and 991 isolated tests.
- **External imagination** as a recurring phrase: the software under test, genAI
  output, and testers themselves all serve as someone's external imagination.
- A Friday-evening PrestaShop session ending "This is exploratory testing", and
  the four-leaf-clover metaphor: "They are there even when you don't find them."

### 3. Test automation and tooling — about 23 percent

Steady from 2020 onward, with the named tools changing underneath.

- **Selenium** (95 posts, concentrated 2022–2025), **Playwright** (46, rising
  every year), **Robot Framework** (28), GitHub Copilot (42, first mentioned in
  2021).
- Robot Framework "makes easy things easier And hard things harder"; replacing
  it with Python and Playwright "made it simpler".
- "Playwright is not one thing" — the experience differs by language binding
  and runner.
- **Decomposition for automation**: steps rather than full flows, functionality
  rather than user experience, then recompose with data.
- **Vendor lock-in**: "Great that you have a tool to take in selenium scripts to
  your commercial platform. Do you also have a tool to take them out?"
- Dynamics 365 test automation as a "learning in the open" thread in 2024–2025.
- **Property-based testing** (autumn 2026): trying Bombadil on an ATM simulator
  exercise gave 24 properties and "found four bugs (that I fixed) and I knew
  only of one of them". The point is not the tool but "the *practical
  property-based testing that Cem Kaner was trying to teach and AI now enables,
  as long as you have the intent*".

### 4. AI in testing and software work — 11 percent overall, 44 percent in 2026

Absent until late 2022, then the fastest-growing theme in the archive.

- **2022–2023, scepticism and integrity.** The carbon cost of training
  ChatGPT; "a significant personal integrity problem with paying in
  organizational scale for licenses to a service based on pillaging digital
  content without consent & compensation", "tilting towards offsetting". A
  Copilot conference demo generating wrong week numbers: "Wake up your inner
  tester or you'll do badly."
- **2024, guardrails.** "Generating tests is not worth it, the time goes into
  READING not writing"; "Generating insights and inspiration is already a
  multiplier." Three sociotechnical guardrails: expected values, an
  anti-toolist worldview, task-wide learning.
- **2025, habits over revolution.** "I'd rather work on habits and
  experimentation." "Read the books and not their summaries. Choose better
  places to be thoughtfully lazy than the core knowledge of your profession."
- **2026, agentic work as daily practice.** Task expansion: "Testers are not
  reporting bugs, they are fixing bugs"; "Testing is the new reading."
  Botsitting, sandboxing ("Yolo should not be the option"), token costs,
  licence ownership between client and contractor, MCPs, building four small
  apps in a day to structure knowledge. "The best of genAI is removal of shame."
- **Autumn 2026, cost and evidence.** A trainee's two weeks on a 20 € Claude
  Code subscription came to an estimated $54.27 of API-priced tokens:
  "Cost-awareness is essential." Commit metrics collected from 2,500 open
  source repositories to see whether AI use shows up in what is produced:
  "Nothing changes if you change nothing." A "2nd brain" built from 38,111
  public notes, with the observation that out-of-the-box LLM analysis has a
  bias "to seek the drama".
- **The human cost noted alongside**: "I feel lonely for talking with AI. I used
  to have these conversations with people." A blog written for twenty years
  turns out to be structured input for agents. In October 2026 (in Finnish):
  when one relationship, the one with the machine, is weighted so heavily, the
  relationships between team members change with it, by accident.

### 5. Community organising — about 26 percent, 66 percent in 2012–2016

The oldest layer of the archive, and the one most specific to LinkedIn.

- **Agile Finland** (89 posts, 2012–2018): Agile Breakfasts, Tampere Goes
  Agile, Scan Agile, Coaching Camp, the Tech Excellence pod and meetup,
  Micropreneurs meetups, executive committee announcements, coding dojos,
  commenting on agile contract terms.
- **Finnish testing associations**: Sytyke's TestausOSY (2011–2016), the
  *Testaus ja Laatu* magazine started in 2012, Ohjelmistotestaus ry, and a 2021
  note that testing now has three homes in Finland ("Testauksella on nykyisin
  kolme kotia").
- **Tivia** (88 posts): board member 2022–2023, monthly Finnish articles for
  IT Insider, Tivia Open Space on a virtual platform, the association's own
  Mastodon server; then the Sytyke board in 2024.
- Testing Assembly in Helsinki, with an open, unsponsored dinner afterwards:
  "Come for the company, stay for the company. You are the company we seek."
- A stated principle: "I do not pay to do volunteer work."
- And weariness with governance: "Olen kyllästynyt johtamiseen" (I am tired of
  leadership) — committees that achieve nothing are full of leaders hoping
  someone else will be the *someone* who does.

### 6. Speaking and conferences — about 29 percent

- **European Testing Conference** (27 posts, 2015–2020): designed and organised
  over five editions in Bucharest, Helsinki, Amsterdam, and Valencia.
- **Selenium**: Project Leadership Committee volunteering, SeleniumConf
  programme work, and an online Selenium Open Space Conference where one way
  to enrol was a pull request to the event page.
- Recurring venues: ATAGTR, EuroSTAR, FroGSConf, NewCrafts, TestMu, SEETEST
  programme committee, Agile Testing Days, PNSQC keynote in October 2026.
- **Counting the work**: 450 talks by mid-2021; 509 talks including 40 public
  keynotes by end of 2022; "47 talk sessions on 30 different topics" in 2021;
  public presentation number 600 in October 2026, delivered from a markdown
  file and demos rather than slides, in a year of 36 sessions.
- **Doubt about the format**: "Talks are others showing off, not testing";
  "Broadcasting from stages is illusion, as is broadcasting on socials." And
  self-knowledge about it: "I have tried stopping public speaking way too many
  times to be taken seriously on that."
- "Speaking / writing about things is my hobby, not my work. My work is what
  drives the continuous need of moving on with talks."
- `#PayToSpeak` and the arithmetic of a promised talk: travel hours, costs
  compensated afterwards, a week away from work and family.

### 7. Teaching and mentoring — about 24 percent

- **#MimmitKoodaa** (2019–2022): free courses for women moving into tech,
  including a remote testing course with a takeaway dinner.
- **#TestingDozen** (2022–2023): a mentee cohort launched at the 25-year career
  mark — a couple of hours a week of learning together, because recruiting no
  longer found modern testers.
- **Teaching by doing together.** "All my teaching has transformed from slides
  to hands-on mobbing." Pairing strong-style with 15- and 16-year-old trainees,
  one of them a conference co-speaker.
- The **text-field exercise** as a long-lived teaching target: "37 inputs and
  the 65 bugs *that I know of*"; "My biggest success is when other people start
  teaching stuff with foundations I have built."
- "Investing in onboarding is the ultimate shift-left" (Anne-Marie Charrett),
  first quoted in 2022 and returned to since.
- Three theses on learning (2025, in Finnish): your career is too important to
  leave to managers and organisations; organisational skill needs call for
  smaller words; the most important learning is learning to learn.
- ISTQB as a recurring foil, from 2012 to an exam coach app built in 2026.

### 8. Ensemble, mob, and pair work — about 8 percent

- Mob programming enters in 2015 with Woody Zuill's visit to Finland; the term
  changes to **ensemble** in 2020 ("people often confused mob with mobile";
  the negative connotations "made joining the practice hard for some").
- **Ten years on**: "I am immensely grateful for the 1000 people I have learned
  from in the last 10 years through seeing how they go about practice and real
  problems." "Thinking does not win, modeling reality wins. You learn testing
  by testing, and seeing others test."
- *Social Software Testing Approaches* as the standing workshop: everyone with
  a computer, pairs with a computer, a group with one computer.

### 9. Agile and delivery practice — 14 percent overall, 54 percent in 2012–2016

- Dominant while the posts are Agile Finland announcements, then fades as a
  word while the practices stay: small batches, release lead times, sprint test
  planning workshops, "how can we make this smaller" instead of "how big is
  this".
- A 2026 Finnish post notes that the "no estimates" movement's most hated task
  is now done with AI.
- "Remembering also what agile gave us, even when we now no longer speak of it:
  smaller batches, smaller risk."

### 10. Career, recruiting, and work identity — about 18 percent

- **Employers as chapters**: F-Secure in the background of the early years,
  Vaisala from spring 2020 (35 posts), CGI from 2024 (74 posts).
- **Recruiting in public**, mostly in Finnish: open positions, "120 applicants",
  subcontractor networks, the observation that two thirds of candidates had
  under five years of experience.
- **Jobs built rather than applied for**: "Mun työpaikat eivät ole löytyneet
  ilmoituksista, vaan ne on rakennettu minua varten" (my jobs were not found in
  adverts, they were built for me).
- "Don't wait for your manager to see all your contributions without your
  help" — writing one's own performance review, which the manager then copied.
- Unicorn roles and "broken comb shapes"; experience as twenty years rather
  than twenty times one year; fractional and interim leadership vocabulary.
- The market for testers in 2026 (in Finnish): one professional now has to be
  test lead, test analyst, and test automator in the same package. "*Fully
  automated, deeply human.*" Both, not either-or.
- Worry for juniors: graduates going straight to unemployment, and no seniors
  later if there are no places to earn their spurs as juniors now.

### 11. Clients, services, and procurement — 10 percent overall, 18 percent in 2026

A theme that barely exists before 2024 and is written largely in Finnish.

- **The buyer's view** (2024): classifying suppliers by how many assistant roles
  their developers and testers need.
- **Public procurement critique** (2025–2026): tendering mechanisms that score
  "quality" as self-declared profile points; quality in these tenders is really
  requirements conformance; real quality comes through supplier reputation.
- **Value beyond the invoice**: "Leaving structures behind that run years after
  I am gone is what I specialize in, and it rarely happens in a gig. I follow
  the tails, for years after."
- Quality across organisational boundaries: more and more reported problems are
  caused by the seams between suppliers.
- Where test automation is bought and placed changes what the same investment
  returns; a colleague's line that automating on the client's side "does not
  feel like influencing quality in the same way" (translated).
- Building a testing team and community of excellence at CGI: "a hand of all
  aces".

### 12. Leadership and agency — about 15 percent

- **Agency** appears every year from 2020: "Agency is something I need to be
  happy. And I want to respect and enable it in others."
- Doing over leading: "Tekeminen muuttaa asioita" (doing changes things).
- Radiating intent; picking up work that "wasn't mine, needs doing".
- Networks as load: "I work with 50 people a week" (2020); 149 unique people in
  a month, tracked with a self-built app (2026).

### 13. Attribution, inclusion, and principles — about 8 percent

- **Attribution**: "Best work wins when you care about the work over the
  credit" — followed by "But that is not how the world works." Founding a
  magazine and feeling "erased and unappreciated"; attribution of words, of
  ideas, and of things that get reused.
- **Being a woman in the field**: counting every "guys"; tone policing; being
  the only woman on a domestic test automation programme; declining a speaker
  series that had found only men for a year.
- Promoting others as a habit: follow-Friday posts, personal Tester of the Year
  nominees, scholarship programmes, PyLadiesCon.
- Six consecutive years on the TiVi 100 ICT influencers list, noted as
  representing software testing rather than as a personal win.

### 14. Writing, blogging, and self-curation — about 21 percent

- LinkedIn as the distribution layer for other writing: `[Blog]` posts, Medium
  articles, IT Insider columns, podcasts, `#talksturnedarticles`.
- **Counting the corpus**: over 700 pieces indexed in 2020; "835 blog posts in
  12 years of blogging. 859K hits" in 2023; 2.04 million hits in 2026.
- The Exploratory Testing Index of 2020, re-run with one request in 2026 after
  five years without time for it.
- `#talksturnedarticles` explained in 2026: most talks are delivered once, so
  each gets an article afterwards.
- The move between platforms is visible: Twitter references until 2023,
  Mastodon from 2022.

Testing is the subject in both (64 and 65 percent). English carries the craft
and the international stage; Finnish carries the associations, the hiring, and
the business of selling and buying testing in Finland.

## Cross-cutting observations

- **One subject, changing lens.** Testing is the majority topic in every period.
  What changes is what it is seen through: agile community in 2012–2016,
  conferences in 2017–2019, exploratory testing and teaching in 2020–2023, AI
  and the services business from 2024.
- **From announcing to thinking.** The early posts tell people where to enrol.
  The later ones work out a position in public and end on a question.
- **Numbers as a habit.** Talks delivered, blog hits, tests integrated and
  isolated, lead times, people talked to per month, bugs known in an exercise,
  tender points. The share of posts using coverage or metric vocabulary grows
  from 3 percent in 2020–2021 to 16 percent in 2026.
- **Community is the method.** Posts credit named people, cite podcasts and
  other people's posts, and ask the network rather than assert conclusions.
- **History as argument.** A 2003 paper, a 2005 syllabus, a 2008 slide, a 2010
  training programme, and a 1979 book are brought forward to show what has
  changed and what has not.
- **Principles stated and paid for.** Not paying to volunteer, not paying to
  speak, choosing which stages to share, and an unresolved integrity problem
  with how AI models were trained.
