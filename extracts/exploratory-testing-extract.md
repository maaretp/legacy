# Maaret Pyhäjärvi on Exploratory Testing

A thematic extract of tweets from [@maaretp](https://twitter.com/maaretp) on exploratory testing, drawn from a personal Twitter archive covering 2010–2022. Curated from roughly 1,300 tweets that mention exploratory testing; this selection favors substantive, quotable, or historically interesting tweets over promotional or duplicate ones.

## 1. What Exploratory Testing Is (Core Concepts)

Across a decade of tweets, Maaret returns again and again to the same core claim: exploratory testing is not a technique or a phase, it's a mindset built on learning, agency and opportunity cost — and it applies to testing whether or not you touch a keyboard, script, or piece of automation.

> Exploratory testing is more of a mindset emphasizing that you need to learn continuously. Every day a little better. Love my work.
>
> — [2013-10-02](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/385468575797882880)

> Exploratory testing is 34 year old word coined to describe the smart testing, skilled work that was common in Silicon Valley - uncommon elsewhere. That’s what I mean with it. https://t.co/cJddpuUuD1
>
> — [2018-07-18](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1019539024824987648)

> Agile testing is the idea that developers can test, and that testers can contribute before development. Exploratory testing is the idea that testing is skilled activity.
>
> — [2018-07-26](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1022462139280310272)

> #ExploratoryTesting is not about unknown unknowns. It is the approach that covers that too but I can just as well explore with known knowns, known unknowns and unknown knowns. Last one, amnesia, is real!
>
> — [2018-08-08](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1027086744422965249)

> All testing is exploratory. Yet, the kind I qualify as intentional exploratory testing is neither short-leash nor low-quality. It’s the smart way of testing that is not at all too common.
>
> — [2019-02-17](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1096997299694325761)

> #ExploratoryTesting is about not separating test design from the execution, thus enabling learning. There’s short-leash and long-leash variants and you can do it with high quality vs low.
>
> — [2019-06-26](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1143948988359598080)

> Exploratory testing is about formulating intent and comparing impact to that that intent and learning from it. Learning the difference of those and understanding both can be off independently helps.
>
> — [2019-10-10](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1182206177289150464)

> Exploratory vs. Scripted Testing. It's what and when we learn that differentiates the two. https://t.co/peMfW2vaMh
>
> — [2020-01-04](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1213453709025386498)

> There’s two parts to exploratory testing: the intentional and the accidental (serendipity). To me ‘ad hoc’ is form of exploratory testing emphasizing the accidental side.
>
> — [2020-01-27](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1221838286097920000)

> Testing without test cases. That’s one way I recognise #ExploratoryTesting. Documentation is the output not the input. Automation is documentation.

> — [2020-05-20](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1263175032190238722)

## 2. Exploratory Testing vs. Scripted Testing / Test Cases as Output

A recurring fight: exploratory testing is often defined only by contrast with 'scripted' testing, and Maaret keeps refining that boundary — landing on the idea that test cases are a valid _output_ of exploratory testing, never a valid input.

> Should exploratory testing really be compared to static code analysis in efficiency? Like comparing apples and oranges.
>
> — [2010-06-06](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/15536753602)

> oops, juha in the trap of scripted vs. exploratory. @michaelbolton would have made good practice for this.
>
> — [2011-11-18](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/137480372551303168)

> Scripted vs. exploratory continuum just doesn't clarify it for me. I often create the script after I've explored. Hands-on with sw first.
>
> — [2012-01-28](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/163280801507721217)

> If test cases are input to your testing, it's most likely not #ExploratoryTesting. With Exploratory Testing, test cases (and documentation in general) is output.
>
> — [2021-01-22](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1352524763395207168)

> Not sure that the opposite of #ExploratoryTesting is scripted testing. It’s more like sticking to plans created prematurely. Scripted testing can be output in end of exploring.
>
> — [2021-04-20](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1384595695135596545)

> @thomrinke @darktelecom I use test cases as input vs output to make sense of this for myself. For non-exploratory, test cases are treated as 80% input and for exploratory, test cases are treated 80% as output. In conversations on exploratory, we could learn from conversations on gender: not binary.
>
> — [2021-07-08](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1413083294774738947)

> As an exploratory tester, there is one thing I create 'test cases' for - bug reports. That's when writing step by step repro instructions may make sense (if I can't avoid it, and I often can).
>
> — [2022-09-01](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1565267229301678081)

## 3. Exploratory Testing and Automation ("Contemporary Exploratory Testing")

From roughly 2018 onward, this becomes the central thread: rejecting the manual/automated split entirely, and reframing automation as something built _through_ exploring, not opposed to it. This is where terms like 'contemporary exploratory testing' and the 'automationist's gambit' come from.

> What's with the idea that I need automation to free my time for exploratory testing? Assumes devs can't do decent quality without automation
>
> — [2015-11-06](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/662678662781779972)

> I’m using exploratory testing to design which tests to leave behind as automated. I feel there’s a book chapter I need to write on that.
>
> — [2018-02-01](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/958999339971604480)

> What makes #ExploratoryTesting and #TestAutomation different (if any) is our fixed beliefs in opportunity cost. We fail to recognize that with modern programming, there's much more examples of programming helping you explore than slowing you down.
>
> — [2018-07-18](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1019465457722683392)

> A difference that isn't real that people assign to #ExploratoryTesting and #TestAutomation is where the bar of done is. Too many times I have latter folks telling me "100% tested" for first group folks to show "here's 20 important problems we are missing".
>
> — [2018-07-18](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1019474151617253376)

> I would have to be a fool to explore for crashes without automation. Exploratory testing is not ‘manual’ and Test Automation isn’t ‘automatic’. People and code in both! https://t.co/n3lLqHmnW8
>
> — [2020-01-05](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1213812722934145024)

> The new tools ‘automating exploratory testing’ are to that goal what IDEs are to ‘automating programming’. And that’s helpful. I love an IDE with great refactoring support. It makes coding more joyful. But we rarely say ‘automated refactoring’.
>
> — [2020-06-11](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1270933453907931136)

> @cm_stead I woke up realizing this is a perfect way to describe what I do with #ExploratoryTesting. I have the automationist's gambit to it. Like with queen's gambit in chess, this has attacking prowess and has requirements for opponent to defend properly. Inspired. Thank you Chris.
>
> — [2020-11-11](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1326400838567407623)

> "You're going to need that exploratory testing on top of automation". No. That exploratory testing feeds into creating that automation. Writing automation while testing does not have to be away from good exploratory testing. Document as automation. Throw some automation away.
>
> — [2020-11-20](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1329824322454773764)

> Automation in frame of #ExploratoryTesting
> ✍️Documenting
> 🎣Extending reach
> 🚨Alerting to attend
> 🔎Guiding to detail
>
> — [2020-12-30](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1344405987462144007)

> #ExploratoryTesting is the pink. It keeps everything else in shape, and fills all the gaps. https://t.co/fhYh5Z968Q
>
> — [2021-04-08](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1380234342706855944)

> We used to think of testing in terms of coverage against requirements. Now, with contemporary exploratory testing, we think of testing in terms of coverage against the answer key to all the relevant bugs.
>
> — [2022-04-30](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1520302216506130432)

> @arslan0644 I have replaced the term with contemporary exploratory testing. Contemporary, because when you explore, you may leave behind executable documentation. You may explore from what others have automated in the team. And you think a lot around writing that little code.
>
> — [2022-08-25](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1562687392758509569)

> Everything that does not need to be automated gets done while automating. #ContemporaryExploratoryTesting
>
> — [2022-08-01](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1553994473918668801)

> ‘Manual’: spends time experiencing the application like users and stakeholders, even on an API level. Focuses on observation. ‘Exploratory’: takes manual further to new info, using code extend to activities humans don’t reach.
>
> — [2020-12-13](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1338046044614119424)

> Comfort in my peers also thinking that the "exploratory testing cloud on top of testing pyramid" and "let's slap some exploratory testing after everything else is done" are ridiculous notions. You can't automate well without exploring. You can't explore well without automating. https://t.co/Kq1bcjsPQG

> — [2022-06-30](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1542452852597170176)

## 4. History and Attribution

## 8. Managing Exploratory Testing (Charters, Sessions, Agency)

A sustained argument against treating session-based test management as the only, or best, way to manage exploratory work — and for agency as the real lever.

>
> — [2018-07-30](https://twitter.com/maaretp/status/1024033765146992642)
