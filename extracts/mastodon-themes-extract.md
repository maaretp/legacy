# Maaret Pyhäjärvi on Mastodon: Themes

A thematic classification of original posts from a personal Mastodon archive
(`archives/mastodon/20260828_outbox.json`), covering November 2022 to August 2026.

## Scope

- 4,935 activity items in the outbox: 4,563 `Create` (own posts) and 372 `Announce` (boosts).
- Of the `Create` posts, 3,519 are original posts and 1,044 are replies.
- This digest classifies the 3,519 original posts.
- Percentages are the share of original posts touching a theme, from keyword
  matching, so they are approximate and posts commonly span two or three themes.

## Core professional themes

### 1. Testing craft and quality — about 45 percent

The dominant, constant thread across every year (40–49 percent annually).

- What testing is and is not; testing as thinking rather than scripts.
- Exploratory testing, charters, heuristics such as SFDPOT, and the observation
  that SFDPOT tends to "collapse to structure and function".
- Bugs, defects, regression; "manual regression testing dies in 6 months".
- Reframing customer questions: "how many tests will you automate" is not a
  request for a number.

### 2. Test automation and tooling — about 12 percent

- Selenium (including Selenium Project Leadership Committee volunteering),
  Playwright, Robot Framework, Cypress.
- "We name one tool but many tools do the work together."
- Flakiness, frameworks, CI, and separating capability-building from
  schedule-critical activity.
- `#selenium` is the most-used hashtag (43 uses).

### 3. Speaking, conferences, and talks — about 13 percent

- Keynotes, CFPs, workshops, slides, live conference notes.
- `#paytospeak` advocacy that speakers should be compensated.
- Recurring venues: FrogsConf, Scan Agile, SeleniumConf, EuroSTAR, FooConf,
  HUSTEF, NewCrafts, TestGuruSummit.
- Highest share early (15–16 percent in 2022–2023), tapering to about 10 percent.

### 4. Teaching and mentoring — about 10 percent

- `#TestingDozen` mentee cohort (41 uses); bidirectional learning, as in
  "TestingDozen teaches ME roman numerals".
- Onboarding people: security consultants, trainees, senior developers.
- "Contemporary Exploratory Testing" course material
  (`#contemporaryexploratorytesting`, 39 uses).
- Summer-trainee retrospectives.

## Workplace and people themes

### 5. Teams, collaboration, and culture — about 12 percent

- Feedback ("feedback is about its giver"), trust, conflict, psychological safety.
- Ensemble, mob, and pair working as a distinct sub-thread (about 2–3 percent).

### 6. Career, hiring, and jobs — about 13 percent, rising

- Grew from about 9 percent in 2022 to about 16 percent in 2026.
- Interviewing, recruiting, layoffs, quitting, contracting, salary.
- Consultant and contractor dynamics, invoice-driven behaviors, detailed
  hours-logging in Jira.

### 7. Management and leadership — about 9 percent

- `#RegretfulManagers` and `#RegretfulManager` as a recurring critical framing.
- Estimation pressure, `#NoEstimates`, `#NoJira`, `#NoProductOwner`.
- One-on-ones, performance reviews, team-size negotiation
  ("knowing your LOC is good for negotiating team size").

### 8. Programming and coding — about 9 percent

- Code review, refactoring, pull-request and commit metrics, Python, Git.
- Frequently from a tester-who-codes angle.

### 9. Requirements and product — about 7 percent

- Specs "grown, not handed over", scope, stakeholders, spikes to remove
  uncertainty.

### 10. Agile, ways of working, process, and metrics — about 3–5 percent

- Scrum, kanban, retrospectives, velocity, deadlines.

## Cross-cutting and personal

### 11. AI and LLMs — about 8 percent overall, growing fast

- Share by year: 3 percent (2022), 8 percent (2024–2025), 20 percent (2026).
- ChatGPT, Copilot, "AI-native delivery", agentic coding, prompting.
- Consistent stance: "getting value out of that stuff is not a tech problem but
  a people problem".
- The fastest-growing theme in the archive.

### 12. Writing and blogging — about 8 percent

- Blog posts, `#TalksTurnedArticles`, book series, newsletters.

### 13. Community and open source — about 6 percent

- `#NewCrafts`, meetups, Selenium project governance, copyleft and licensing,
  volunteering.

### 14. Finland and the Finnish tech scene — about 5 percent

- Helsinki meetups, TIVIA, Vaisala, some Finnish-language posts.

### 15. Diversity, gender, and inclusion — about 3 percent

- Women in tech, PyLadiesCon; overlaps with management critique.

### 16. Personal and non-work — about 3–4 percent

- Reading and book series, weekends, being sick, recognition moments
  ("LinkedIn legend"), musings on kids' photos and privacy.
- Notably sparse; this is a near-purely professional account.

## Trends over the archive

- Testing craft is a rock-steady core at about 45 percent.
- AI went from a footnote to roughly one in five posts by 2026.
- Career, hiring, and jobs roughly doubled in share.
- Conference and speaking declined as a share, though absolute activity stayed high.
- Management critique (`#RegretfulManagers`) ticked back up in 2026.
